# Prices from subcontractors

> Put a scope out to the subs you want a number from, and see what comes back side by side. They answer on a link — no sign-in, and they see only the scope you write.
> **Route:** /dashboard/m/jobs/*/bids
> **Order:** 270

## What this is

You write one scope — *"Electrical: 200 amp service, rough and trim, excludes
fixtures"* — pick the subcontractors you want a price from, and send each of
them a link. They open it, type their number, and it appears here.

**Nothing is emailed for you.** You get a link and you send it the way you
already talk to that sub: a text, your own email, a message. Each one gets
their own link, so you can see who has looked and close one without touching
the others.

## Putting a scope out

{button:Ask for prices|primary|plus} asks for four things:

- **What is being priced** — the trade, as you would say it.
- **Cost code** — optional, and worth setting: if you walk an estimate later,
  the winning number goes straight onto that phase.
- **Wanted by** — the date you want numbers. Links stay live a week past it,
  because a sub who answers the morning after is one whose number you still want.
- **The scope** — what is in, what is out, anything they need to price it.
  **This is all they see.** Not the estimate, not the other bidders.

Then {button:Ask somebody|outline|plus} for each subcontractor. Their link is
copied to your clipboard as soon as it is made — paste it straight into
whatever you are sending.

## What comes back

Each row shows the subcontractor and where they have got to:

- {badge:waiting} — sent, not opened.
- {badge:opened} — they have looked and said nothing.
- {badge:bid|secondary} — they gave a number, and it is shown.
- {badge:no bid} — they looked and said they are not bidding. Worth having; it
  means stop waiting on them.
- {badge:revoked} / {badge:expired} — the link closed before they answered.

The line above the rows counts them and gives **both ends of the range**. That
spread is the thing to read: three prices within a few per cent means everyone
understood the scope the same way, and one at half the others means somebody
read it differently — worth a phone call before it becomes the cheapest bid on
your estimate.

### The buttons on a row

{button:Go with this|primary|trophy} marks the one you are taking. Only one per
scope, and only on a row that actually has a number. It does not buy anything —
it decides which figure your estimate uses. Buying the work is still a purchase
order or a subcontract.

{button:Copy link|ghost|copy} gets their link again, to re-send.

{button:Close their link|ghost|ban} stops that one sub getting in, without
touching anybody else. **Anything they already said stays** — closing a link
takes back access, not what they told you.

{button:Stop taking prices|ghost} closes the whole scope. Existing links stop
working; the numbers you have are kept.

## How it reaches an estimate

Give the scope a cost code, award one of the bids, and then
[walk an estimate](walking-an-estimate.md) over that phase: it puts the winning
number on as one line, marked {badge:their bid|secondary} with the
subcontractor's name. A phase you are subbing is one lump, not a breakdown of
somebody else's work.

If nobody has been awarded, the walk will not guess — not even the lowest bid.
Which one you are going with is your decision.
